It isn’t the surgery. It’s the fifteen minutes before it.

Cataract surgery itself is one of the most common, most refined procedures in medicine.

What trips people up is the lens decision that comes with it — and it's permanent. Here's what that can look like:

  • More nighttime glare and halos than you expected
  • Still reaching for reading glasses you were told you might not need
  • Never knowing which questions to ask, because you'd never heard the terms before

That's usually nobody's fault. It's a math problem.

Fifteen minutes is not enough time to absorb words like "toric" and "extended depth of focus" for the first time — and make a decision you'll live with for the rest of your life.

That's the gap ClarityIQ closes — not by telling you what to choose, but by making sure you understand what you're choosing between, before the lens is implanted.

What changes the moment you begin

Every scattered search, replaced by one real conversation

No more sorting through contradictory forum threads at 11pm, wondering what you're missing. One guided conversation, built around your specific goals — not a generic search result — leaves you with real answers instead of more open tabs.

A straight answer, with no reason to steer you

No commission. No bonus. No quota. A board-certified surgeon with no financial stake in which lens you choose — so what you hear is built to help you understand, not to move you toward any particular option.

Built from a curated library, not the open internet

ClarityIQ isn't a general chatbot improvising its way through your questions. Every fact it can share — every lens option, every consideration — comes from a single library built and personally reviewed against FDA documentation and clinical sources, not the open internet.

That's what keeps the conversation accurate instead of improvised: it can only tell you what's actually in that library.

One of the only education experiences built entirely around IOL decisions — not a general assistant repurposed for healthcare, and not left to guess when a question falls outside what's known.

What you walk away with

  • A full walkthrough of your vision goals — what matters most to your day-to-day life
  • Education on cataract surgery itself — what to expect before, during, and after
  • A complete review of every IOL option available to you
  • A guided walk-through of your personal pathway among those options
  • As much time as you need to ask questions and go deeper on what matters to you

And you walk away with a personalized summary of your goals — plus customized questions to bring to your surgeon.

Why it helps to do this before the lens is implanted

Once your natural lens is replaced, that choice is permanent — no six-month exchange like a bad pair of glasses. Use ClarityIQ before your consult, after it if questions remain, or as a second look. The earlier you talk it through, the more time you have to sit with what you learn and decide what to ask next. There's no upside to waiting until the lens is already in.

Frequently asked questions

Is this medical advice?

No. ClarityIQ is an educational conversation, not a diagnosis or treatment recommendation. Nothing here replaces your surgeon's evaluation of your eyes and your medical history. This is education to use at any point before the lens is implanted, not a substitute for that conversation.

Will it tell me which lens to get?

No — and that's intentional. We don't have access to your eye exam, your medical history, or the clinical details that go into that decision. What we can do is make sure you understand your options well enough that the conversation with your surgeon is easier and more productive.

Is this actually AI?

Yes. You'll be speaking with a guided AI conversation, not a human. We tell you this before you begin, because we think that's the honest way to do it — not something to bury in fine print.

What happens to what I say?

Nothing is recorded or stored. No audio, no transcript. Your summary is generated, shown to you once, and emailed to you once — we don't retain the conversation or the summary after that.

What's the difference between monofocal and multifocal lenses?

Monofocal lenses focus at one distance — most people choose distance, and use reading glasses for close-up work. Multifocal lenses are designed to provide a fuller range, including near vision, but come with a greater chance of noticing halos or glare at night. Neither is "better" — it depends on what matters most to your daily life.

What is a toric lens, and do I need one?

Toric lenses are built specifically to correct astigmatism — without one, astigmatism isn't addressed regardless of which other lens category you choose. Whether you need one depends on your specific eye, which is exactly what your surgeon confirms at your exam.

Do advanced lenses mean I'll never need glasses again?

Not necessarily. Even with the most advanced lens, you may still want glasses occasionally for one specific task — that's a normal outcome, not a sign anything went wrong.

This page is education, not medical advice. It doesn’t diagnose anything or tell you which lens to choose, and it doesn’t make ClarityIQ your doctor. Your surgeon decides with you, based on your exam.
