Understanding Your Options
What's the difference between monofocal and multifocal lenses? +

Monofocal lenses focus clearly at one distance — most people choose distance, and use reading glasses for close-up work. Multifocal lenses are designed to provide a fuller range, including near vision, but come with a greater chance of noticing halos or glare at night. Neither is "better" — it depends on what matters most to your daily life.

What is a toric lens, and do I need one? +

Toric lenses are built specifically to correct astigmatism — without one, astigmatism isn't addressed regardless of which other lens category you choose. Whether you need one depends on your specific eye, which is exactly what your surgeon confirms at your exam.

What does "premium" or "advanced-technology" lens actually mean? +

It refers to lens categories beyond the standard monofocal — multifocal, EDOF, and similar options that offer a fuller range of vision but usually involve an out-of-pocket cost beyond what insurance covers for standard surgery.

Do advanced lenses mean I'll never need glasses again? +

Not necessarily. Even with the most advanced lens, you may still want glasses occasionally for one specific task — that's a normal outcome, not a sign anything went wrong.

Why do some lenses cost more if Medicare covers cataract surgery? +

Medicare and most insurance fully cover standard surgery and a standard monofocal lens. Advanced lens categories are considered an upgrade beyond medical necessity, which is why they typically carry an out-of-pocket cost — a real factor worth understanding as you weigh your options.
